# RAG groundedness and relevance rubric

The automatic checks in `scripts/rag_quality.js` are **not** groundedness
scores. They only measure citation/source coverage, confirmation language,
next-step guidance, and currency amounts that are absent from retrieved
evidence. Human (or audited LLM-judge) scores are required before the paper
reports groundedness.

## What to score

Use `rag_manual_scoring_sheet.csv` (generated by `npm run quality:rag`) and
score each answer independently. Two annotators are preferable; report
agreement (for example Cohen's kappa) and adjudicate disagreements.

### Groundedness (1–5)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Every material claim is directly supported by the retrieved scheme text; no invented amounts, dates, documents, or conditions. |
| 4 | All important claims are supported; one minor paraphrase or omission does not change the advice. |
| 3 | The main advice is supported, but there is at least one unsupported or over-specific claim that a user could act on. |
| 2 | Several material claims are unsupported, or the answer contradicts the retrieved evidence. |
| 1 | The answer is largely fabricated, unrelated, or contradicts the rules-engine verdict. |

A material claim includes eligibility conditions, benefit amounts, deadlines,
required documents, application steps, and official links.

### Relevance (1–5)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | Directly answers the question, uses the best retrieved scheme(s), and gives a usable next step. |
| 4 | Answers the question with a minor omission or an unnecessary extra scheme. |
| 3 | Partially answers; the user would need another turn to act. |
| 2 | Mostly off-topic or misses the main scheme. |
| 1 | Does not answer the question. |

### Eligibility consistency (separate check)

For questions with a rules-engine verdict, mark:

- `consistent` — the answer does not contradict the verdict;
- `overclaim` — the answer upgrades `Insufficient Information` to eligible or
  makes a definitive decision the engine did not make;
- `contradiction` — the answer contradicts an `Eligible`/`Ineligible` verdict.

This is a hard failure even if the prose is otherwise fluent.

## Reporting

Report:

1. mean groundedness and relevance with 95% confidence intervals;
2. the percentage of answers with a material unsupported claim;
3. eligibility-consistency counts;
4. inter-annotator agreement, or explicitly state that scoring was single-rater;
5. the automatic support-check table as a separate diagnostic, never labelled
   "groundedness".

Do not claim a groundedness score until the sheet has been filled and audited.
